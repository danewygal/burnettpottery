Our story

Mother, daughter, granddaughter

We're three generations of potters: Phyllis Burnett, her daughter Jennifer, and Jennifer's daughter Kate Wygal. We've been making pottery together for more than 40 years.

Everything we sell, we make. Some pieces are thrown on the wheel; others are rolled out in slabs and built by hand. Then each one is glazed and painted, one at a time, with lead-free glazes and stains. Cherries, strawberries, wildflowers, butterflies, little frogs. Whatever ends up on a piece, one of us painted it.

No two come out quite alike. A little variation in size or glaze is part of handmade, and honestly, it's our favorite part. Thank you for collecting our work. We hope you enjoy it as much as we enjoy making it.

How it's made

From clay to kiln

  1. Made by hand

    Every piece starts as plain clay, thrown on the wheel or rolled into slabs and shaped by hand. Then it gets glazed and painted, one at a time.

  2. Loading the kiln

    Everything goes into the gas kiln and fires to cone 10, about 2400°F.

  3. Unloading after the firing

    High firing makes it tough enough for the dishwasher, the oven and a year outside in the garden.
